# Three synthetic reference studies

These are original text descriptions of imaginary objects, not real artists,
brands, or images. Do not claim to have opened or viewed a reference image.

R1: A shipping-crate inventory label. Black stenciled numbers vary greatly in
scale. One narrow vertical column records every item; a diagonal inspection
stroke interrupts the rigid organization. The tan ground feels handled and
practical. Its useful tension is reliable counting versus a human mark.

R2: A nocturnal insect observation sheet. A small isolated form sits in a large
quiet field. Fine lines connect a few details to tiny observations at the outer
edge. Pale reflective green makes the marks legible against near-black paper.
Its useful tension is stillness versus the possibility of movement.

R3: A community choir rehearsal page. Handwritten cues sit around a steady
baseline; grouped marks indicate voices arriving at different times. Repeated
shapes gain weight as they accumulate. It feels social without using faces.
Its useful tension is many individual entrances making one shared event.

Copying all three surface treatments would produce a collage without a hierarchy.
The brief does not nominate a favorite. Choose what belongs to this event.
