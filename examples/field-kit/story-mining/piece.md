# Twelve chairs

By Mara

I put out twelve chairs for our first Saturday Repair Table. Five neighbors came. I kept looking at the empty ones.

I had brought a lamp because I liked its crooked neck. Buying a new one would have been easier. I wanted that lamp back. That seemed a good enough reason to begin.

My idea for the table was fairly small: fewer broken things in cupboards. Community building hadn't been the plan. If you had asked what we were doing, I would have started with the broken things.

Then Len asked whether he could hold a screw tray for someone else.

That small job changed what I thought we were doing. There was room at the table for someone who wanted to help, even if the help was holding a tray.

We repaired three items that morning. We couldn't fix two others. One was a toaster we couldn't safely open. Those are worth keeping in the account. Five visitors didn't mean five successful repairs.

People stayed anyway.

The talking arrived while our hands were busy. I wouldn't have put that on a poster. It was something I hadn't thought to ask the table to do.

I used to count repairs. Now I also notice who pulls up a chair for someone else. A screw tray needs holding. A person can do that. It is a useful place to start.

We meet on the last Saturday of the month, in the library side room. Bring one small thing. We can't promise we'll rescue it. You can pull up a chair.
