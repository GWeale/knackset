# Twice the admissions, twice the screenings

*An offline investigation of the fictional Lark Cinema source packet. All linked sources below are supplied fictional excerpts, not retrieved web pages.*

The Lark Cinema's operating return contains two doublings. In January–September 2026, admissions rose from 4,000 to 8,000 compared with the same months of 2025. Public screenings rose from 40 to 80. Once both figures are in view, the owner's claim that the Midnight Club doubled attendance becomes much harder to establish.

The attractive explanation begins with the [owner's October 12 newsletter](https://example.invalid/lark/newsletter): a club starts in January and brings an old cinema back to life. The next day's [Arts Desk article](https://example.invalid/arts-desk/lark) repeats that account. But it quotes the newsletter and supplies no independently collected attendance data. These are two appearances of the same claim.

The [October 20 operating return](https://example.invalid/lark/operating-return) supplies the useful comparison:

| Programme and period | Screenings | Admissions | Admissions per screening | Available seats used |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| All screenings, Jan–Sep 2025 | 40 | 4,000 | 100 | 66.7% |
| All screenings, Jan–Sep 2026 | 80 | 8,000 | 100 | 66.7% |
| Midnight programme, Jan–Sep 2026 | 30 | 3,300 | 110 | 73.3% |
| Other screenings, Jan–Sep 2026 | 50 | 4,700 | 94 | 62.7% |

*Calculations use the return's capacity of 150 per screening; percentages are rounded. The last row is the total less the midnight programme.*

The average screening attracted exactly as many admissions in each year. The small calculation that matters is:

> 40 additional screenings × 100 admissions per screening in 2025 = 4,000 additional admissions.

That equals the entire recorded increase. A larger programme at the previous average audience is therefore sufficient to account for the doubling arithmetically. This does not identify what caused the cinema to add screenings, or whether those screenings would have filled without the club.

The club does have a substantial place in the story. Its 30 screenings generated 3,300 admissions, averaging 110 each against 94 for other screenings that year. That is evidence of a well-attended strand within this programme. It is not a matched comparison: the packet provides no controls for film choice, timing, promotion, or the audiences different screenings could attract. Nor can all 3,300 club admissions be treated as additional visits caused by the club. Some visitors might otherwise have attended another screening. The return does not tell us.

A second explanation becomes plausible when the timeline widens. [Evening buses resumed on January 5](https://example.invalid/transit/evening-service). A [council notice published February 4](https://example.invalid/council/lark-grant) reports that accessibility and heating restoration was completed in February, allowing the venue to open on more evenings. These changes overlap the club's first year. The restoration offers a concrete route to increased programming; resumed buses offer a plausible route to easier access. Neither source measures how many screenings or admissions its change produced. The evidence supports considering them alongside the club, not assigning them numerical credit.

There are two further limits to the headline. The return compares nine-month periods, so it cannot verify the newsletter's annual claim. And an admission means a ticket used. Eight thousand admissions could include many repeat visits; the number of distinct people reached is unknown. Business recovery also remains unmeasured in this packet.

The supported conclusion is that the Lark doubled its recorded admissions while doubling its screenings, with the Midnight Club contributing a large, relatively well-attended part of the expanded programme. The owner's stronger causal story remains unproved. The useful next question is how the additional screenings became possible and drew their audiences. Dated programming records and evidence about displaced visits could distinguish a club-led expansion from an expansion enabled by restoration and transport. Those missing observations could change the attribution; another article repeating the newsletter could not.
