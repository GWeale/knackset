## Checks performed

- Compared both admissions and screening denominators across the same nine-month periods: each doubled; admissions per screening stayed at 100.
- Calculated seat use from the stated 150-seat capacity: 4,000 ÷ (40 × 150) and 8,000 ÷ (80 × 150), each 66.7% rounded.
- Subtracted the club from 2026 totals: 80 − 30 = 50 other screenings; 8,000 − 3,300 = 4,700 other admissions; 4,700 ÷ 50 = 94 per screening. Club average: 3,300 ÷ 30 = 110.
- Tested the scale explanation: (80 − 40) × (4,000 ÷ 40) = 4,000 admissions, equal to the total increase. This is an arithmetic reconciliation, not a causal estimate.
- Traced the repeated headline to its single stated origin, reconstructed the timing of club, transport, and venue changes, and checked annual versus nine-month scope and admissions versus people.

## Rival explanations and remaining uncertainty

- **Club-led growth:** Consistent with the owner's account and the club's higher average attendance. The record lacks a comparison showing what would have happened without it.
- **Expanded opportunity:** Strongly consistent with twice as many screenings at the same overall average attendance. Restoration explicitly allowed more opening evenings; transport also changed. Their individual effects remain unmeasured, and the club could itself have motivated expansion.
- **A larger population reached:** Untested. Repeat visits could contribute to the doubled admissions; neither their size nor distinct visitor growth is known.

The main unresolved issue is attribution among overlapping changes. A dated schedule could show when expansion occurred; attendance by programme and evidence of visitor substitution could test whether club visits were additional. Financial data would be needed to assess the separate claim of business revival. No external checking was attempted because this is a bounded supplied-source exercise.
