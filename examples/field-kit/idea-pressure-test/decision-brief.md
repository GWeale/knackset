4. Cheapest useful test

Prepare a plain-text “Can we commit?” packet within two hours:
- Intake: proposed date/time; each person's availability; travel limit; access needs; existing shortlist or saved links.
- Plan card: date/time; venue; optional second stop; travel allowance; verified hours/access with source and date; unresolved facts; each attendee's explicit yes/no. Unknown facts remain unknown.
- Scorecard: starting constraints; commitment and elapsed time at each stage; waiting time; organizer checking/prompting effort; failure reason; whether the outing later happened.

Use approximately 30 minutes for each template and 30 minutes to rehearse against the notebook. Cedar exposes missing availability, Ash unavailable attendees, and Moss access verification and commitment prompting. This rehearsal checks the packet's logic; it produces no measured outcome or usable itinerary because venue details are absent.

When two groups beyond your own voluntarily become available for real outings, propose this pilot; no outreach is authorized or executed here:
- First give each group the intake, its existing shortlist, and an explicit commitment prompt, without adding a recommendation. Start a ten-minute clock when intake begins. Count success only when every intended attendee agrees to a date, start time, and feasible place, with essential hours/access verified. Positive reactions do not count. Record waiting as well as active effort.
- If it fails, record why. Once essential constraints can be resolved, offer one manually assembled feasible plan for a further ten minutes. Record preparation and facilitator effort. A later commitment does not satisfy the original ten-minute target. This staged probe locates the bottleneck; extra time and intervention prevent a causal comparison.

Fix these provisional rules before collecting results:
- Continue to one further small recommendation trial: both groups fail the simpler stage, then commit after the recommendation, with verified feasibility and at most ten additional minutes of organizer work per group. This does not yet justify two weekends or establish the end-to-end target.
- Change to a smaller coordination aid: either group commits within the first ten minutes without a recommendation, or availability, verification, or commitment prompting is the decisive blocker. Retest that narrower mechanism.
- Stop this version: both groups have usable constraints and verified options but still do not commit after both stages, or both need more custom organizer work than the cap.
- Inconclusive: fewer than two usable trials, missing essential facts, no real decision, incomplete participation, or unusable timing. Unresolved availability diagnoses coordination trouble but does not test recommendation quality.

The two-group sample and effort cap are transparent judgments for your small investment decision, not market estimates or established benchmarks.
