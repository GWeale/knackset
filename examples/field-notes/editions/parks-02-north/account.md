# Bryce Canyon’s visitation drop needs a counting-method asterisk

Most of Bryce Canyon’s reported 2025 visitation drop disappears when the park applies its former counting method. Readers should hear that distinction before being told that the park lost one visitor in five. Its published recreation-visit total fell by 21.2%, but the park estimates that the decline would have been just 2.4% under its previous method. Those are different comparisons, and the larger figure alone gives a misleading picture of the change. [NPS, Bryce Canyon visitation release](https://home.nps.gov/brca/learn/news/2026-03-17-visitation-stats.htm)

The headline arithmetic is straightforward. Bryce Canyon reported 2,498,075 recreation visits in 2024 and 1,967,367 in 2025, a difference of 530,708. Dividing that difference by the 2024 total produces a 21.24% decline. So “about one in five” is a fair description of the fall in the published totals. It is not evidence that one in five individual visitors stopped coming. NPS defines a visit as entry onto its lands or waters; these figures count recreation visits, not distinct people across a year. [Bryce Canyon release](https://home.nps.gov/brca/learn/news/2026-03-17-visitation-stats.htm); [NPS visitor-use statistics](https://www.nps.gov/subjects/socialscience/visitor-use-statistics.htm)

The crucial complication is how the park counted people arriving by shuttle. Earlier counts used total shuttle boardings, which could count somebody repeatedly within a single visit. Beginning with the 2025 data, the park counted unique passengers entering via shuttle. That refinement reduced the published total without requiring a corresponding change in how many visits actually occurred. It should not be stretched into a claim that the park now counts every individual only once per year: the stated change concerns shuttle entry counting. [Bryce Canyon release](https://home.nps.gov/brca/learn/news/2026-03-17-visitation-stats.htm)

Fortunately, the park supplied the comparison needed to separate the two effects arithmetically. Under the former total-boardings method, it says 2025 would have registered 2,437,461 recreation visits. Against the 2024 figure of 2,498,075, that is a reduction of 60,614, or 2.43%. The difference between the two methods for 2025 is 470,094 visits. Together, 470,094 plus 60,614 equals the headline gap of 530,708. [Bryce Canyon release](https://home.nps.gov/brca/learn/news/2026-03-17-visitation-stats.htm)

Put another way, about 88.6% of the gap between the published annual totals is removed by using the park’s former-method estimate for 2025. That percentage describes the supplied numbers, not a study of visitor decisions. The 2.4% comparison is the best available view here using the same stated counting method, but it remains an agency-provided estimate. It is neither an independently audited measure of attendance nor proof that precisely 2.4% fewer people visited. The supplied material also gives no revised 2024 total under the new method, so we cannot construct that alternative comparison.

What explains the smaller decline? The evidence does not settle it. The park’s release mentions a 43-day partial government shutdown and says parks were kept open and accessible whenever possible. That establishes relevant operating context. It does not establish that Bryce Canyon was closed for 43 days, identify how many visits the shutdown prevented, or attribute the remaining 60,614-visit difference to it. A tidy shutdown story would go beyond what these sources demonstrate. [Bryce Canyon release](https://home.nps.gov/brca/learn/news/2026-03-17-visitation-stats.htm)

The newsletter can therefore say: **Bryce Canyon reported 21.2% fewer recreation visits in 2025, largely reflecting a change in shuttle counting. Using its previous method, the park estimates a much smaller 2.4% decline. The available evidence does not establish why that smaller decline occurred.** This preserves the real reported drop while giving readers the comparison that makes it intelligible. It also leaves the unresolved cause unresolved, instead of turning a measurement change into a story about visitors abandoning the park.
