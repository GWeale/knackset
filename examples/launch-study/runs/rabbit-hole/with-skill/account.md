# What Knackset’s 10/10 can tell you

The most revealing detail in Knackset’s 10/10 result is that the two agents on the losing side still saved the right Gmail labels. Nothing was sent. Their failure was narrower: after writing, they reread the message they changed but omitted an independent check of the starred message they were supposed to preserve. The study caught a gap in verification, even though every saved state was correct. That is useful evidence. Its subject matters. [Study report](../../../inputs/evals/results/comparison-final-20261101/REPORT.md.txt)

Put that score beside 25 skills and the launch brief’s 133 passing helper tests, and a larger story becomes tempting: the collection has proved that its creative methods improve an agent. The supplied evidence cannot sustain that conclusion. The consequential mismatch is a change in what Knackset is offering. Its comparative evidence belongs to office workflows; its new invitation concerns creative judgment.

The published alpha described 16 skills for mail, office tasks, research and browsers, accompanied by a synthetic local runner. It reported 120 helper and fixture tests and the same 20-run comparison: 10/10 with a skill, 8/10 without one. It explicitly declined a general superiority claim and said live-service testing remained pending. The alpha did not establish the sweeping promise a reader might now infer from its headline score. [Alpha snapshot](../../../inputs/published-alpha.md.txt)

Follow the score to its denominator and the distinction sharpens. Ten successes means ten executions across two repeated tasks: five Sheets runs and five Gmail runs. Sheets finished 5/5 in both variants. All ten Sheets agents handled the persisted-write timeout by appending once and independently reading the saved rows. The only disagreements concerned those two Gmail verification reads. The report also discloses unblinded judging, setup and launch-order deviations, and unknown cost. Its observations support a bounded account of behavior under those fixtures; they do not measure originality, explanatory insight or the usefulness of a creative direction. [Study report](../../../inputs/evals/results/comparison-final-20261101/REPORT.md.txt)

The current front door has moved. It invites users to investigate strange stories, remix references and decide whether an idea deserves months of work. The evidence index separates 16 older utilities from nine draft field-kit methods: 16 + 9 = 25 packages, not 25 demonstrated improvements. A helper-test total concerns checks of supporting software. Even accepting the brief’s 133 passes, adding more such tests cannot answer whether a method improves a finished investigation or poster. The supplied packet contains no current test transcript with which to audit that number. [Current README](../../../inputs/README.md.txt) · [Evidence index](../../../inputs/evals/results/README.md.txt)

There is a stronger alternative to the idea that the creative direction is merely packaging: inspect what it has produced. The examples index identifies actual agent outputs from fictional raw material, including a poster, investigation, curve experiment and generative edition. Night Orchard’s editable SVG records three supplied textual reference studies and says no reference images were available. These are concrete things a prospective user can examine and adapt. They give the methods more substance than an attractive description alone. [Examples index](../../../inputs/examples/README.md.txt) · [Night Orchard](../../../inputs/examples/field-kit/reference-remix/night-orchard.svg)

Yet an artifact leaves the central comparison open: would the same agent, given the same brief without the method, have done as well? The examples index expressly says there were no comparison baselines. The current README also says the older offline records do not validate the creative methods. This weakens a story of concealed failure or careless numerical inflation. My reading is a transfer problem: evidence accumulated for an earlier purpose remains nearby as the collection changes direction. The documents already draw the boundary; the next release needs to make it easy to see.

An honest release can say: “Knackset offers nine draft creative and investigative methods with inspectable worked examples, alongside 16 utilities with scoped offline records. A small earlier Gmail/Sheets comparison completed 10/10 with skills and 8/10 without; it does not establish creative improvement.” Keep any helper-test count attached to the software checks it actually reports.

The observation that would change this conclusion is a repeated advantage on varied creative tasks in fresh, matched with-skill and no-skill runs, with blinded assessment of the resulting artifacts against criteria fixed beforehand. That would begin to connect method to benefit. Until then, the useful invitation is to try a promising method on work you care about and inspect what changes.
