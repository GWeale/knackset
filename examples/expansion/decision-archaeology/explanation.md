# Why matching groups stay collapsed

The supplied records strongly support a workshop interaction decision as the original reason: filtering should preserve the facilitator's chosen session arrangement. Rendering performance is a later explanation with some later measurement behind it; it is not the documented reason for adopting this behavior.

At the exported head, `b41d620`, filtering removes groups without matching cards and narrows cards within the remaining groups. It does not open a matching group: `isOpen` comes only from `state.expandedGroupIds` (`board-source.txt`, `ui/board/groupVisibility.ts`, lines 01–13, especially line 10). A collapsed group still has a match count, and its `Cards` collection is not rendered (`BoardView.tsx` excerpt). This is a source inspection, not a live observation or an executed test.

The strongest alternative explanation is performance. It is plausible mechanically because collapsed groups do not render their card collections, and task 203 later measures a difference. But the adoption-era review explicitly treats performance as a separate future investigation. The supported conclusion is that facilitator control and continuity drove the recorded adoption; performance can be an additional reason to consider today. The supplied records cannot establish that nobody privately considered performance, or explain exactly why the handbook later presented it as the reason. Calling that wording a deliberate rationalization would go beyond the evidence.

## The tradeoff to revisit

The team accepted weaker discovery of matches inside collapsed groups in exchange for preserving the facilitator's arrangement and avoiding an unwanted return to an earlier exercise. Counts and manual opening were the chosen mitigation. A separate results panel was also considered, but locating a panel result back on the board had not been designed (`board-records.md`, review options 1–3). The product comment explicitly left hidden-match discovery open for later reconsideration.

The underlying need is session control and continuity; keeping groups collapsed is the selected mechanism. Automatically opening groups may now be worthwhile if the search workflow or user needs have changed. That change in premise has not been established by these records. Also, restoring saved expansion when search clears is insufficient by itself: the original prototype already did that, and the disruption occurred during search.

Before changing it:

1. **Repeat the consequential workshop scenario.** Start with a previous exercise collapsed and the next activity open; search for a card in the previous exercise, inspect it, and clear search. Compare the current interaction with the proposed automatic opening. Observe the presenter's place/scroll position, focus, participants' interpretation of the active exercise, and whether counts/manual opening prevent people from finding needed matches. This directly checks whether the original need still applies and whether the proposed benefit outweighs the disruption. The export contains no post-ship workshop evidence that the chosen remedy worked.
2. **Specify the expansion semantics before implementation.** Decide whether automatic opening is a temporary display override or a persistent change to saved choices, and what an explicit toggle during filtering means after the query changes or clears. Preserve explicit choices unless the new product decision intentionally changes that contract. Add or update a public behavior check for the chosen sequence. The supplied preservation test documents the current contract; it was inspected, not run (`board-source.txt`, `groupVisibility.test.ts` excerpt).
3. **Measure the proposed behavior on a representative board and device.** Task 203 is a useful baseline, with a 7 ms median difference in its one fixture, but it covers a local laptop, short text, and no collaborators. It excludes touch devices, long bodies, images, remote cursors, and screen sharing, and makes no interaction recommendation. Use the actual proposed expansion behavior under relevant conditions before accepting or rejecting it on performance grounds. Its largest samples are not established worst-case bounds.

The smallest useful next step is the bounded workshop comparison in item 1, with temporary versus saved expansion made explicit. If auto-opening helps discovery while preserving place and session understanding, there is evidence to revisit the mechanism. If it recreates the observed confusion, keep the continuity requirement and explore a way to reveal results without disrupting the board, such as the previously deferred results-panel alternative. Performance measurements should constrain that choice; they should not substitute for checking the interaction problem.
